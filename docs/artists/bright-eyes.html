Bright Eyes — No One Would Riot for Less

Played: 2026-07-09

From Cassadaga (2007), Bright Eyes' major-label breakthrough named after the Florida spiritualist town where the band did research for the record. A slow-building, socially pointed track that showcases Conor Oberst's more expansive, string-and-choir-backed arrangements from this era, a step up in scale from his earlier lo-fi work.

Bright Eyes — Wonderful Life

Played: 2026-07-30 (TikTok)

Conor Oberst's Bright Eyes recorded "Wonderful Life" as a cover of the Felice Brothers' original, contributing to the tribute compilation Felice County Fair released 16 October 2026 via Sony. Oberst has been a longtime champion of the Felice Brothers, previously collaborating with them and even launching a record label that released their music. He said of them: "Whatever little check marks in my heart need to be checked to make the perfect music, they check all those things." The compilation also features covers by The Lumineers, Old Crow Medicine Show, Nathaniel Rateliff, Dawes, Brandon Flowers of The Killers, Bebe Stockwell, and Deer Tick. The Felice Brothers wrote the original "Wonderful Life" on their album From Dreams to Dust (2023).

  • TikTok angle: Opens on Oberst's quote about Felice County Fair and his longtime championing of the Felice Brothers, previewing Bright Eyes' cover for the upcoming tribute compilation.

Bright Eyes — Lua

Played: 2026-08-13

Bright Eyes is the Nebraska-based project of Conor Oberst. "Lua" (4:31) is track 4 on the band's sixth studio album, I'm Wide Awake, It's Morning, released 25 January 2005 on Saddle Creek Records (catalog LBJ-72). "Lua" was released as a single on 26 October 2004 on Saddle Creek (LBJ-68; Saddle Creek Europe 7" SCE 68), ahead of the album. In November 2004 the singles "Lua" and "Take It Easy (Love Nothing)" took the top two positions on the Billboard Hot Singles Sales chart.

  • Sources: Wikipedia · Discogs · Genius
