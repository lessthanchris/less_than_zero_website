Creedence Clearwater Revival — Fortunate Son

Played: 2026-03-12 (TikTok)

Track 3 on Willy and the Poor Boys, released 2 November 1969 on Fantasy Records; also released as a double A-side single with 'Down on the Corner' in September 1969. Written by John Fogerty in under 20 minutes in a single burst of frustration at the Vietnam draft; Fogerty thought of figures like David Eisenhower while writing. Described as the only popular antiwar song of the 1960s to explicitly focus on class privilege in the draft process. Peaked at number three in the US. Fogerty publicly objected when the song was later used in a Wrangler jeans commercial without his consent. Rolling Stone ranked it number 99 on their 500 Greatest Songs list.

  • TikTok angle: Fogerty wrote 'Fortunate Son' in under 20 minutes in 1969, about the sons of senators and presidents dodging the Vietnam draft while working-class kids were shipped out — angle: different wars, same deal, 57 years later.

Creedence Clearwater Revival — Proud Mary

Played: 2026-09-03 (Moment in History)

John Fogerty wrote it in 1968 on the eve of his Army discharge, turning what started as a song about a "cleanup lady" into an anthem about a steady riverboat. Released January 1969 on Bayou Country, it reached No. 2 on the Billboard Hot 100. Its longer cultural afterlife is the fact that Ike & Tina Turner's radically rearranged 1971 version became equally iconic. Enshrined in the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame's "500 Songs That Shaped Rock and Roll."

  • Moment in History: Ike Turner — famous for that radically rearranged cover of this very song with Tina Turner — was released from prison in 1991 having served 18 months of a four-year term, stemming from a 1990 conviction for driving under the influence of cocaine following a string of late-1980s drug arrests. He told Variety he'd spent over $11 million on cocaine, never beat his addiction, and died of a cocaine overdose on 12 December 2007.

  • Sources: Wikipedia · Rock & Roll Hall of Fame

Creedence Clearwater Revival

  • Origin: El Cerrito, California, USA · formed 1959 (as the Blue Velvets; became CCR in 1967) · disbanded 1972
  • Members: John Fogerty (lead vocals, lead guitar, primary songwriter), Tom Fogerty (rhythm guitar), Stu Cook (bass), Doug Clifford (drums)
  • Genre: Swamp rock / roots rock, with blues, R&B, and country influences
  • Discography:
  • Creedence Clearwater Revival (1968) — Fantasy Records
  • Bayou Country (1969) — Fantasy Records — featured "Proud Mary," US No. 2
  • Green River (1969) — Fantasy Records — US No. 1
  • Willy and the Poor Boys (1969) — Fantasy Records — featured "Fortunate Son" / "Down on the Corner"
  • Cosmo's Factory (1970) — Fantasy Records — US No. 1
  • Pendulum (1970) — Fantasy Records
  • Mardi Gras (1972) — Fantasy Records — final studio album before the split
  • Facts:
  • Despite being active as CCR for barely four years (1968–1972), the band has sold more than 45 million albums and singles in the US alone.
  • In 1969 alone they released three major albums and headlined Woodstock — an extraordinary output rate driven almost entirely by John Fogerty's songwriting.
