From Remember The Humans (2026), released on Arts & Crafts. During the recording sessions, both Kevin Drew and producer David Newfeld lost their mothers. The album was named in the aftermath of that.

Broken Social Scene are a Toronto collective — a rotating cast built around Kevin Drew and Brendan Canning, with a membership that has included Feist, Emily Haines of Metric, and Amy Millan of Stars. Broken Social Scene — Hey Amanda

Played: 2026-03-19

Track 9 from Remember the Humans, the band's first studio album in nine years, due 8 May 2026 on Arts & Crafts / City Slang; released as the second single 12 March 2026. Reunites the Toronto collective with producer David Newfeld, who also produced You Forgot It in People (2002) and their self-titled album (2005). Both Newfeld and co-founder Kevin Drew lost their mothers during the recording sessions. Features contributions from Feist, Lisa Lobsinger, and Hannah Georgas. The band described the song as being 'about being yourself, and how people will always question that.' The album announcement coincided with news of a joint summer tour with Metric and Stars, three Canadian artists with deep shared histories dating to the early 2000s.

Broken Social Scene

  • Origin: Toronto, Ontario, Canada · formed 1999 · active (intermittently)
  • Members: Core duo Kevin Drew (vocals, guitar) and Brendan Canning (vocals, bass), backed by a rotating collective whose ranks have run from 2 to 15+, including Justin Peroff, Andrew Whiteman, Charles Spearin, Leslie Feist, Emily Haines (Metric), Amy Millan (Stars), and others
  • Genre: Indie rock, post-rock, baroque pop
  • Discography:
  • Feel Good Lost (2001) — Arts & Crafts
  • You Forgot It in People (2002) — Arts & Crafts / Paper Bag — Juno Award winner, widely regarded as a landmark Canadian indie record
  • Broken Social Scene (2005) — Arts & Crafts
  • Forgiveness Rock Record (2010) — Arts & Crafts
  • Hug of Thunder (2017) — Arts & Crafts
  • Remember the Humans (2026) — Arts & Crafts / City Slang
  • Facts:
  • Arts & Crafts, the label most of the band's catalogue is on, was founded by Kevin Drew and Jeffrey Remedios specifically to release You Forgot It in People.
  • The collective's membership has been fluid enough that it's been described as running from as few as 2 to as many as 15 musicians at once, drawing from Toronto's Metric, Stars, and Feist orbit.
